# Arsenal

**Use it: [junkdrawer.works/arsenal](https://junkdrawer.works/arsenal/)**

**Every pitch in big-league baseball: how it's held, how it spins, how hard it's thrown and where it ends up.** Pick a pitch and it's thrown from the mound to home plate, seen from behind the plate, from the side or from above, with a ghost of the same throw with no spin so you can see what the spin did. Next to it are the grip, the ball turning on its real spin axis, and the 2026 Statcast numbers for that pitch.

## How it works

- **Thirteen pitches** in four groups: fastballs (four-seam, sinker, cutter), breaking balls (slider, sweeper, curveball, knuckle curve, slurve), off-speed (changeup, splitter, forkball) and oddities (knuckleball, eephus).
- **The throw.** Each pitch leaves a right-hander's hand (or a left-hander's) at its average speed, release height and extension, and is pushed by its average spin-induced movement on top of gravity and air drag, so it reaches the plate in the right time with the right break. The ball spins at its real rate the whole way, and the dots along its path are where it was every 1/30 of a second, so the farther apart they are, the faster it's going. Watch at real speed, slowed to a quarter, or to a twentieth.
- **The ghost** is the same throw with no spin. The gap between them at the plate is the movement Statcast measures. A dashed ring marks the ball 0.175 seconds from the plate, about when a hitter has to decide whether to swing.
- **Throw it with another pitch** to see two pitches leave the same release point together and split late, the way pitchers pair them.
- **The grip**, drawn as the pitcher sees it from behind, with see-through fingers so the seams show underneath.
- **The spin**, shown 40 times slower, from behind the pitcher (with a clock for the tilt) or from the side. A tap on the ball stops it and starts it again. The seam pattern follows the pitch: four seams a turn for a four-seamer, two for a sinker, the red dot of a slider's bullet spin.
- **The whole arsenal**: every pitch on one movement chart, and a table of how often each is thrown, how hard, and how often hitters miss it.
- **Pitch to a hitter.** Pick a pitcher (a league-average righty, or one of nine real 2026 arsenals, from Paul Skenes to Tyler Rogers's submarine sinker and Matt Waldron's knuckleball) and a righty or lefty batter. Call each pitch and one of 25 spots, the strike zone's nine boxes (framed on the grid) and the ring around them, and throw it; from a keyboard the pitches and the spots are one Tab stop each, picked with the arrow keys. Pitches miss their spots by a realistic amount, and each comes in at its own speed. Each one is marked where it crossed the plate, with what happened in a tag of its mark's colour over the picture (a pitch that crosses on top of an earlier one leaves a ring of the earlier one's colour showing round it), and why: what the hitter was looking for, whether he picked it up, and where and when he swung. The at-bat ends with a scout's note on what decided it. "What would a catcher call?" suggests a typical call for the count, another each time it's asked, and says what big-league pitchers and hitters did in that count in 2026, "Throw each of his pitches 400 times from here" charts what every pitch in the arsenal does per 100 throws from the same count and spot, and what matters in that count: strikeouts with two strikes, walks with three balls, otherwise strikes and balls (once the at-bat is over, it charts the count and spot of its last pitch: what else he could have thrown there), and "Copy link to this at-bat" makes a link that replays it exactly on the same version of the hitter. A link carries that version: one made against an earlier hitter still opens and replays, on today's, with a line under the pitch log saying the same calls may play out differently. The address keeps that link as you pitch, so reloading the page picks the at-bat up where it was, and a phone's Share sends it. A link that arrives cut off, or with a full stop or bracket from a message on the end, replays as much as can be read and says what was left out.
- Links go straight to a pitch, like [junkdrawer.works/arsenal/#sweeper](https://junkdrawer.works/arsenal/#sweeper), and keep the pitch thrown with it and a left-hander: [#sweeper+sinker.lefty](https://junkdrawer.works/arsenal/#sweeper+sinker.lefty) is a lefty's sweeper thrown with his sinker. No account and nothing saved. It works offline and installs to a phone's home screen.

## The numbers

Every number is from Statcast, through Baseball Savant's search, for the 2026 regular season: 713,311 pitches. Averages weight each pitcher by how many of that pitch he threw. The knuckleball's count only the pitchers who threw at least 100 (in 2026, Matt Waldron alone), which leaves out position players' lobs; how often it was thrown, and by whom, still counts everyone. "Spin moves it" is the movement from spin alone (Statcast's induced vertical break and horizontal break); "Drops" is the total drop from release, gravity included. `node tools/statcast.mjs 2027` fetches a new season into `js/numbers.js`; the words in `js/pitches.js` quote a few of the 2026 figures, so read them over after a refresh.

The flight is a constant-acceleration model, like the one Statcast fits to each pitch: the speed, movement and release come from the data, and so does where it's thrown: each pitch lands where a right-hander's pitch of that kind crossed the plate on average to right-handed batters in 2026 (Statcast's plate_x and plate_z; the knuckleball's from Waldron's alone), mirrored for a left-hander. `node tools/statcast.mjs --aim` fetches only those aim points. The tests check that the model's total drop comes out within 12% of Statcast's (or 3 inches, whichever is more) for every pitch.

## The hitter

The at-bat is a coaching model, not physics, built on the same flight model. Before each pitch the hitter guesses fastball or not, from the count and what he expects of this pitcher: the pitcher's 2026 mix, and what he has thrown so far in the at-bat and what followed the last pitch earlier in it, so the same pitch every time, or the same two in turn, gets figured out. Then he sits on one pitch. He reads the ball until the decision point, 0.175 seconds from the plate. If it isn't the pitch he's sitting on, he may still pick it up: less often when it comes out of the same tunnel as the pitch before. If he doesn't, he decides and swings as if it were the pitch he expected: where that pitch would cross the plate, and when. So a slider that looks like a fastball at the decision point is chased off the plate, and a changeup he takes for a fastball gets him out in front. Changeups and splitters get chased more than other pitches, and curves taken more in the zone, as in 2026. Where a pitch crosses matters as it did in 2026: one on the corners is missed almost twice as often as one over the middle, and one that crosses where earlier pitches in the at-bat did gets hit harder. A miss now and then is a foul tip, a strike in any count. Every pitch comes in at its own speed, spread about the pitcher's average as his were in 2026.

The strike zone is 2026's: the plate's width, between the season's average top and bottom of the zone (1.62 to 3.22 feet; in 2026, the first season of the automated ball-strike challenges, each batter's zone was set from his height), and a ball touching any edge of it is a strike. The umpire's zone shifts with the count, as 2026's umpires' did: more strikes called at 3-0 than at 0-2.

His knobs are tuned by `tools/calibrate.mjs` against the 2026 league, gathered by `tools/league.mjs` from Statcast: how often hitters swing in each of the 12 counts and miss when they do (foul tips count as misses, as Statcast counts them), how much of their contact they foul off in each count, how often taken pitches are called strikes in each count, how often they chase pitches out of the zone and miss those, how each kind of pitch gets swung at, chased, missed and lands in the zone, misses and damage by where pitches cross, and what happens on balls in play. Each real pitcher's pitches are tuned to his own miss rates, and his strikeouts to his own. His timing is tuned too: in 2026 a pitch right after one 6 mph faster or slower was missed no more often than one right after the same pitch, so it isn't pulled toward the last pitch's speed. Over thousands of at-bats it comes out within a point or two of the league on walks, swings, chases, misses, fouls, called strikes and hits; strikeouts run within a point of the league's (22% against 22%), and at-bats are as long, 3.9 pitches. Thrown down the middle every pitch, one pitch to one spot every time, or the same two pitches in turn, a pitcher does clearly worse than with his own calls, with two exceptions: Matt Waldron's knuckleball, whose wobble the hitter can't read however often he's seen it, does better to one spot than his own calls, and a two-pitch closer's two pitches in turn do about as well as his own calls, which are much the same thing (Mason Miller's four-seamer up and slider down and away strike out 45%, against his real 47%). `test/atbat.test.mjs` holds it there.

## Running it

It's a static site: plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript modules, with no build step.

```sh
npm start                     # serves it at http://localhost:8080 (any static file server will do)
npm install                   # esbuild and upng-js, for the one-file copy in npm test and the tools below
npm test                      # the flight and at-bat models against the numbers, then the real page and the one-file copy in Chromium (needs Playwright)
node tools/screenshots.mjs    # redraws docs/*.png and og.png
node tools/make-icons.mjs     # redraws the PNG icons from icon.svg
node tools/statcast.mjs       # refreshes js/numbers.js from Baseball Savant
node tools/league.mjs         # refreshes js/league.js, the at-bat's targets and the real pitchers' arsenals (about ten minutes; --cache=DIR keeps the downloads)
node tools/calibrate.mjs      # retunes the at-bat model to js/league.js and writes js/atbat-params.js
npm run build                 # dist/arsenal.html, the whole thing in one file
```

The retuning keeps its best round, fits each real pitcher's pitches last so nothing after undoes them, and then puts the knobs to the same checks as `test/atbat.test.mjs`, on three seeds pooled: if any fails, it says which and writes nothing. It is seeded (seed 1, or `--seed=N` for another search) and dated by `js/league.js`, so the same league numbers and the same starting knobs write the same `js/atbat-params.js`, byte for byte; `--out=PATH` writes it somewhere else, to compare two runs. It starts from the knobs already in that file, so a second run into it moves them again, and new knobs change how old at-bat links replay: raise `VERSION` in `js/atbat.js` with them, and the pinned links in `test/atbat.test.mjs` say when to.

To put it online with GitHub Pages: **Settings → Pages → Build and deployment → Deploy from a branch**, then pick `main` and `/ (root)`.

### Files

- `js/pitches.js`: the thirteen pitches: names, grips, how each works. `js/numbers.js`: the Statcast numbers, where each pitch is aimed among them, made by `tools/statcast.mjs`.
- `js/flight.js`: the flight model and the three cameras with their scenery. `js/stage.js`: the animation.
- `js/ball.js`: the baseball, drawn from the real seam curve with 108 stitches. `js/grip.js`: the grips. `js/spin.js`: the spin panel and tilt.
- `js/charts.js`: the movement chart and the table. `js/app.js`: the page. `js/math3.js`: small 3D helpers.
- `js/atbat.js`: the at-bat model: the hitter, the count, a pitcher who calls his own pitches, the runs behind the 400 throws, and the at-bat links, written and read. `js/atbat-params.js`: its tuned knobs. `js/league.js`: the league numbers it's tuned to and the real arsenals. `js/atbat-words.js`: what each pitch did and why, in words. `js/atbat-ui.js`: the section on the page. `js/tracker.js`: the pitch marks, the batter's box and the at-bat's view, fitted to the screen. `js/rng.js`: the seeded random numbers that let a link replay an at-bat.
- `fonts/`: Barlow and Barlow Condensed (SIL Open Font License), served from here so nothing loads from elsewhere.
- `sw.js`: keeps a copy for using offline, and opens from it when the connection is up but not answering. `FILES` in it is a fingerprint of the files it keeps, so a release replaces the whole copy at once; the e2e test says what to set it to.
- `test/atbat.test.mjs` plays every pitch of every pitcher to every spot, replays an at-bat from its seed, writes and reads the links of 2,000 at-bats called at random, at every pitch of each, and reads links cut off or with something on the end, replays five pinned links pitch by pitch, so a change to how the hitter plays fails until his version goes up, checks the count's rules, and runs the lab (`test/lab.mjs`): thousands of at-bats on three seeds pooled, measured against the league overall, in every count (swings, misses, fouls and called strikes), for each kind of pitch (misses, swings in the zone and chases), by where pitches crossed (misses and wOBA on contact), and for each real pitcher's pitches and strikeouts, plus that a changeup after a fastball fools him more than one after a changeup, that throwing down the middle every pitch, one pitch to one spot or two in turn does clearly worse than a pitcher's own calls, and that Miller's four-seamer up and slider down and away in turn strike out under 50% on every seed, and that 400 throws of each pitch from one count and spot put the same pitch on top whatever the seed. It reads the words of thousands of at-bats, the pitchers' own calls and those 2,000 random ones, for what reads badly: idioms, American spellings, a decision point blamed when the pitches were far apart or the same kind, his timing blamed when he wasn't out in front, and weak contact said the wrong way round for where the pitch crossed; and it checks that every speed in a real pitcher's line is one of his pitches', and that this README's strikeouts are within a point of the lab's and the league's and its count of pitches is the season's.
- `test/physics.test.mjs` checks that every pitch is aimed where Statcast's crossed the plate, that every one but the knuckleball, which wobbles, lands there, and that the gap to its ghost is the Statcast movement; that every pitch's drop is close to Statcast's; the flight times; the tilt clock; that the at-bat's view marks a pitch at the ground and shows the pitcher's hand on a phone, a computer and a tablet; that the paths, the ghost, the strike zone and the chalk are as thick on a phone as on a computer; and that the newest mark grows in as it lands and a mark covered by a later one keeps a ring showing. `test/e2e.mjs` picks pitches every way the page offers, throws them from every camera, pairs two and checks what the caption says about them, checks that the paths stand out on the light sky, switches hands, dismisses the movement chart's tooltip with Escape, stops the spinning ball, pitches to a hitter by tapping on a 390px and a 320px phone and on a phone held sideways and with the keyboard's arrow keys, checks what a screen reader hears for each pitch, reloads partway through an at-bat, opens at-bat links with a full stop or a bracket on the end, cut off or garbled, or made against another version of the hitter, and #Sweeper in capitals, checks that no line of the log starts with its separating dot at 390 and 320px, that the count and what happened over the at-bat's picture never cover a pitch thrown high and off the plate (at 320, 360 and 390px, sideways and on a tablet, to either batter) and that what happened is in a tag that reads at 4.5 to 1, that no green line shows along either picture's bottom edge on a computer, that the dark palette's stadium lights are seen whole, clear of the words, that the call fits under the at-bat's picture from the first pitch to Throw for every pitcher on an iPhone 14 with Safari's bars tucked away (390x750), that Aaron Nola's "Knuckle curve" keeps to one line at 320 and 360px, that the buttons are reached 44px tall without a neighbour taking the tap and the lists are in 16px words, which an iPhone doesn't zoom into, that two pitches' times stay clear of the legend at 320, 360 and 375px, that the time over the picture is written only when it changes, that a pitch picked far down the page waits at the release until the picture is back in view, and that the lede's link goes down to Pitch to a hitter; it asks Chrome whether it can be installed, opens an at-bat link on a tablet either way up and on a computer in both palettes, and checks it opens offline, opens from its copy within 8 seconds when the connection stalls, and takes a new release's copy from the server. It serves the site under /arsenal/ the way GitHub Pages does, with a ten-minute cache.
- `test/single.mjs` builds the one-file copy and opens it the way a host shows one, written into a frame on another page that may run scripts and nothing else: the lede's link goes down to Pitch to a hitter without the frame leaving the copy, an at-bat plays to its end, and nothing goes wrong on the way.
