Pitcher

How it works

    Pitch to a hitter

    The hitter guesses what’s coming, and when he’s fooled he swings where the pitch he expected would have gone. Over thousands of at-bats he swings, chases and misses about as often as big-league hitters did in 2026, and each real pitcher’s pitches get missed about as often as his did, and he walks and gets hit as he did.

    Call the pitch

    Pick a pitch and a spot, then throw it. The hitter has to commit at the decision point (the dashed ring on each pitch’s path), 0.175 seconds from the plate, and a swing at a pitch out of the zone is a chase. Strikes are red discs (called, swinging or fouled), balls green rings, balls in play blue squares.

    Where

    The nine inside the frame are the strike zone; the ring around them is outside it.

        Be the hitter

        A big-league pitch gets to the plate in about 0.4 seconds, and a hitter has to know what it is before then. Watch one come in from behind the plate and name it.

        Name that pitch

        One of his pitches, at its own speed, the way he throws it to a batter on your side: only the ball, with nothing to say what it is. Then say what it was.
